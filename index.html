Evgeny Redekop

Experimental physicist and builder at heart — I work with quantum systems and physical observables, and build AI and software tools to analyze and control them.

Email Google Scholar GitHub LinkedIn CV (PDF)

I did my PhD at UC Santa Barbara in Andrea Young's group as an NSF Quantum Foundry Fellow, and I'm now a Thouless Postdoctoral Fellow at the University of Washington, working with the groups of Charlie Marcus and Matthew Yankowitz.

For years I built ultra-sensitive scientific instruments — custom hardware and cryogenic systems designed to pull tiny signals out of a lot of noise — work that led to results published in top journals, including Nature. It taught me how to turn messy, real-world data into answers you can actually trust.

I bring the same instinct to software. I build human-in-the-loop AI tools and agents that plan experiments, control instruments, and make sense of complex data — and I care about the same things I did at the bench: get a clean signal out of a noisy system, and never trust a number you haven't checked. I move easily across hardware, data, and code.

I'm at my best at the edge of what's known, learning a new field from zero, and I'm drawn to ambitious, early-stage problems. Always happy to talk through new ideas.

Three Voices — a collaborative AI analyst

AI · research tooling

A human-in-the-loop AI system for analyzing experimental data. Instead of a black-box "agent that runs everything," it works the way a good collaborator does: it proposes one analysis step at a time into a live notebook you co-edit, defends its choices, and gets corrected. The scientist owns every conclusion.

One exchange from a real run, turn by turn.

  • AI proposes, scientist owns — graduated autonomy, never auto-gated.
  • Adversarial figure-judge — scores each figure blind, the analyst gets a right-of-reply, the judge reconsiders with the actual code to catch wrong-computation bugs.
  • Teaching channel — a one-keystroke correction becomes a durable lesson that reshapes the system's future behavior.
  • Persistent memory — a literature index, open questions, and run-by-run findings that carry across sessions.

Built solo over ~3 weeks. Source is currently private; happy to walk through the design and to discuss new ideas.

Dissipation at the superconductor–insulator transition

physics · transport

An ongoing study of an Al/InAs Josephson-junction array in a dilution refrigerator, asking how controlled dissipation reshapes the superconductor–insulator transition and the anomalous-metal phase at its boundary. I run the campaign end-to-end — measurement, gate-space mapping, and a BKT / dissipation-exponent analysis — much of it through the AI analyst above, which is what makes a one-person campaign tractable.

Research

Quantum instrumentation bridging condensed matter and quantum information — nanoSQUID-on-tip microscopy, spatially resolved spectroscopy, and cryogenic low-noise measurement.

nanoSQUID-on-tip microscopy

instrumentation · imaging

A superconducting loop a hundred nanometres across, grown on the apex of a pulled quartz tube and scanned tens of nanometres above a sample. It is sensitive enough to resolve a single vortex of magnetic flux, and the same sensor reads out four quite different quantities depending on what you point it at.

Each panel is a cartoon of a measurement the tip actually makes, with the paper it came from underneath.
